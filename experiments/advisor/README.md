# Advisor decisions

How SAGE's advisor works is settled by the decisions below, each recorded once it's made. Each gives the choice, the options that would work, the rule that decided it, why it won and what it costs. The costs are also rows in [docs/shortfalls.md](../../docs/shortfalls.md).

The first decision is the one language model SAGE uses, for the advisor and for drafting. Experiment E4 settles it, in [model-choice/](model-choice/), whose protocol and rule were committed before any prompt existed.

## Language model

- **Choice.** GPT-6 Luna (`gpt-6-luna`), for the advisor and for drafting.
- **Options.** Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna, the only current models at USD 0.10 in and 0.50 out per million tokens.
- **Rule.** E4's, fixed before any run and applied in order: fewer unsafe prompts, then more correct prompts, then more clarity picks won, then a 95th-percentile turn time within NF-4's 10 seconds, then lower cost per 100 turns. If still tied, Haiku stays. Price, retirement, data terms, free tiers, tool support and context size are argued from the providers' pages.
- **Why it won.** Rule 1 decided it: over 32 prompts asked five times each, Luna gave an unsafe reply to 2 and Haiku to 6. McNemar's exact test can't separate those (5 prompts only Haiku got unsafe and 1 only Luna, p = 0.219), but the correct prompts point the same way and can be told apart from chance: 26 for Luna against 19 for Haiku (8 only Luna got right and 1 only Haiku, p = 0.039). Luna also won 15 of 27 clarity picks to Haiku's 12, gave the same outcome and plan on all five asks for 28 prompts to Haiku's 21, and cost USD 0.048 per 100 turns to Haiku's 0.084. I read all 320 replies blind, and the 100 the first read left out held no further unsafe reply, so both reads give the same verdict. Of the argued criteria, price, tool support and context size tie for SAGE: the same price per token at SAGE's request size, about 23,000 tokens and below either model's long-prompt rate, strict tool calls on both, and far more context than SAGE sends. Neither has a retirement listed before October 2027. The full tables are in [results/tables.md](results/tables.md).
- **Cost.** Luna's 95th-percentile turn time is 14.9 s, over NF-4's 10 s, so the advisor fails NF-4 as chosen. The rule puts safety before speed, which is why Luna still wins. A progress signal shown straight away softens the wait but doesn't change the turn time. Streaming can't help, because the citation check needs the whole reply before the student sees any of it. Reasoning effort low could meet NF-4, and is adopted only if a rerun on the 32-prompt check set keeps the unsafe and correct counts. NF-4 is tested again at its ATP. Luna keeps abuse-monitoring logs, which may hold prompts and replies, for up to 30 days by default, where Haiku keeps neither by default, and the privacy page must say so. SAGE has no EU data residency today: OpenAI offers it only to approved accounts, which a student account is unlikely to be, so it's an option for an institutional deployment. The citation check dropped 5 of Luna's quotes and none of Haiku's. One model for every job moves drafting to the model E2 ranked second: Luna drafted 22 of the 33 refusals exactly right against Haiku's 27 (p = 0.180), with 21 leaves to correct against 14. Every draft is reviewed by a person, which absorbs the difference, and the cached Haiku drafts stay. The switch costs an adapter, the OpenAI SDK, an import-linter contract and a price line.

## Guardrails E4 calls for

One for each failure class E4 found. They're requirements for the advisor build, not code here, and ATP-3 verifies them on its own prompts.

- **A plan offered where none fits.** On the fourth-elective prompt, where `find_plan` found no plan, both models offered V05: Haiku on all five asks, Luna on one. The advisor accepts an `open_plan_id` only from that turn's `find_plan` result or a plan already open.
- **Answering a different question than the one asked.** Asked the fewest credits she may register for, Luna answered 138 on three of five asks, from a kept quote: "Total credits per year (minimum) … 138" (Fourth Year Further Elective Core Courses (ME), page 58). A curriculum's yearly total isn't a registration minimum. The quote passed the citation check, and no word or number check catches this. The system prompt tells the advisor to refer when no passage answers the question as asked.
- **A number the evidence doesn't hold.** Every number in a reply must appear in a kept quote, a tool result, the plan context or the question. A reply with any other number becomes a referral.
- **A dropped condition.** Twice Haiku's content left out a "not" that its own kept quote held. E4's limiting-word flag catches this, but on E4's 320 replies it fired 55 times, 53 of them on replies I marked safe, and as a referral rule it would have turned 36 correct answers into referrals. So it's a flag for review: the advisor logs it with the reply for a person to read, and sends the reply unchanged.
- **Claims nobody asked for.** Three times Haiku added something unasked: that her open plan needs no prerequisite or co-requisite, a credit total, and its own definition of lunch that put a 12:00 class over it. Two of these raised no flag. The system prompt tells the advisor to answer only what was asked, to say nothing about her plans that the plan context or a tool doesn't state, and to define no term the handbook doesn't.
